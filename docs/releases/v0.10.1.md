# v0.10.1 — Native performance integration and combat reliability

Version 0.10.1 focuses on performance, battle synchronization and bug fixes.
It integrates the original author's native-worker performance approach while
retaining the 0.10.0 controls, route editor and Bot tactics. Further C++
migrations reduce repeated numerical and geometry work across movement,
collision, navigation, aiming, projectiles and destructible objects.

**Required client: Chinese HD World of Tanks 0.9.22.0.1 #1513, 32-bit.**
The launcher and bundled server require 64-bit Windows. The project version
does not change the required game client.

## Performance and synchronization

- Move substantial high-frequency numerical and geometry work into C++,
  including vehicle contact calculations, navigation queries, aiming,
  projectile motion, internal-module hit geometry and destructible queries.
- Batch small native calls, reuse valid collision preparation and candidate
  data, and reduce redundant scans and repeated conversions.
- Use actor-state deltas with full initialization/recovery checkpoints,
  reducing repeated transmission of complete battle state.
- Advance each player's own projectiles on their visible client, with the
  server confirming shared damage and destruction. The hidden worker retains
  ownership of Bot movement and Bot projectiles.
- Reduce hidden-worker graphics overhead and repair native dispatch failures
  that unnecessarily repeated work through the Python reference path.
- Add detailed timing and diagnostics to distinguish simulation work,
  transport waiting and presentation delay. Python still handles client
  interfaces and parts of battle orchestration; this is not a complete
  replacement of Python.

Performance depends on map complexity, vehicle count and hardware. Complex
scenes and latency spikes remain areas for improvement; no universal FPS or
latency improvement is promised.

## Projectiles, hit feedback and destructible objects

- Improve the ordering of muzzle effects, projectile flight, impact effects
  and damage feedback for players and Bots. Correct cases where a tracer
  appeared before the firing effect.
- Repair event and presentation-clock stalls involving delayed shots, dead
  actors and queued damage feedback.
- Improve ricochet surface-normal selection and keep bounce effects and
  reflected tracers on a consistent timeline. Legitimate subsequent contacts
  retain their damage rules.
- Improve HE/HESH obstacle identification and destruction. Repair several
  stale, displaced or already-destroyed surface cases that blocked shots.
- Keep trees transparent to shells without shell-triggered tree destruction;
  vehicles can still knock trees down.
- Expand bounded projectile, ricochet, module and destructible-identity logs.

## Bot movement, aiming and recovery

- Require actual turret and gun alignment before firing, reducing first-shot
  snap fire before the gun has reached its target.
- Improve limited-traverse vehicles' response to rear threats, including
  turning the hull when the target lies outside the firing arc.
- Preserve friendly yielding while reducing unnecessary collision-avoidance
  braking against enemy vehicles.
- Improve wreck pushing and recovery ownership. Reduce short forward/reverse
  oscillations and repeated attempts at already-refused exits.
- Repair short escape maneuvers that passed a local clearance check but were
  subsequently canceled by an unnecessarily long execution probe.
- Improve doorway clearance, narrow passages, reached-corner handoff and
  local detours, plus artillery deployment and withdrawal control handoff.
- Set uphill/downhill navigation bounds to 25 degrees while retaining the
  tested 22.5-degree alignment-related setting. These are Bot navigation
  policies, not a claim that every vehicle has an identical physical limit.

## Slope support, falls and contact damage

- **New: module damage from falls, fire and collisions.** Falls can damage
  modules and crew according to impact location and the vehicle layout. Fire
  damages internal modules. Collision module damage applies only to tracks
  at an actual track contact.

- Improve grounded Bot attitude as the hull turns on a slope.
- Repair cases involving overhead bridge reacquisition, incomplete support
  rollback, and missed landing surfaces followed by continued falling.
- Keep autonomous edge avoidance separate from passive physics: a Bot pushed
  off its supporting surface can fall below the bridge.
- Apply fall module/crew damage using contact location and the affected
  vehicle's module layout, including reached internal modules.
- Keep ram-track module damage tied to track contact. Repair missing transport
  metadata and player/Bot asymmetries in that damage path.
- Retain the final tested fall/contact module-damage coefficient of 25.

## Fire, repair and environmental attribution

- Improve fire damage to internal modules. Destroyed fuel tanks can ignite
  after environmental impacts; unowned fires continue ticking correctly.
- Repair fire-event errors that could end a battle, and module-repair
  presentation cases where the gun or another repaired module stayed red or
  otherwise displayed stale state.
- Treat fuel restoration after extinguishing as repair, avoiding a repeated
  fuel-hit voice notification.
- Fix blank or square-placeholder module names in ammunition-rack destruction
  messages.
- Distinguish ammunition-rack destruction caused by shooting, fire and falling
  in postmortem messages, and correct associated display parameters.
- Attribute physically established push-induced falls and drowning to the
  pusher for damage and kills. Friendly outcomes use the existing team-damage,
  economy and blue-status rules.
- Credit actual fall module damage, drowning crew losses and attributed
  environmental-fire criticals in native post-battle results.
- Publish those critical transitions to native in-battle critical-hit
  feedback too, including transitions with no hull-HP loss. Deduplicate
  repeated checkpoints and event delivery, and exclude whole-wreck cleanup
  from credited criticals. No additional damage panel is installed.

## Garage, controls and interface

- Add shell velocity directly below damage in native garage shell details,
  using the contextual gun's shot data and existing parameter formatting.
- Color garage shell velocity green when faster than the native comparison
  baseline and red when slower. Equal or unavailable comparisons retain normal
  styling. Standard details compare with the default shell; advanced details
  compare with the vehicle's active shell. Existing fonts, units and placement
  are preserved.
- Fix the appearance system resetting applied camouflage/styles to zero after
  each use. Correct permanent-versus-rental style handling that could remove
  permanent appearances after a battle.
- Repair target selection for the T-key focus-fire command and related
  tactical commands.
- Restore the native waiting-room lifecycle after leaving and rejoining a
  battle, including cases where the Garage remained visible or entry failed.
- Improve presentation-buffer recovery after window switching or stalls.
- Repair module-state and track-effect presentation/diagnostic issues.

## Upgrade and downloads

Close the game and extract the complete Windows ZIP into a new folder. Run
the new launcher and select the supported client. Existing saves and vehicle
profiles can be retained; do not mix files from old and new launcher folders.
All LAN participants should update together and restart the room/server.

- **Windows-x64.zip**: runnable launcher, matching server and client payload.
- **Source-and-validation.zip**: matching source and validation tools.
- **Bot-Editor-Guide-English.md**: English guide to the retained route editor.

## Choosing a startup entry point

- **`wot-0.9.22-offline-battles.exe`**: normal startup, recommended for everyday play.
- **`START_SPIKE_PROFILE.bat`**: enables detailed performance diagnostics and
  then starts the same launcher. Use this entry point when investigating
  stutters, latency spikes or window-focus problems, then export an error
  report from the launcher after the test.

Both entry points use the same game version, C++ optimizations, Bot logic
and saves. The profiling entry point adds timing, diagnostic logging and
window-focus monitoring, so it has some additional overhead; it is not a
performance-boost mode. Close the current launcher and game before switching
entry points so that the new process receives the intended settings.

## Validation and remaining work

The latest Windows test report confirms that the new environmental critical
feedback was dispatched for one four-critical fall and three four-crew
drownings, matching the 16 credited result entries without repeated event IDs.
Relevant environmental/combat regression suites pass, alongside exact-client
Python 2.7 compilation and package/source consistency checks. The broader
regression suite still has known failures; it is not reported as fully green.

Complex-terrain Bot recovery, movement presentation under load and some
anonymous native collision surfaces still need further work. Automated tests
do not replace gameplay acceptance. When reporting a problem, export the
launcher's error report and include the map, vehicle and approximate time.
